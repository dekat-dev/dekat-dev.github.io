Privacy Policy for Dock

Last updated: October 17, 2026

Dock is developed by Dekat ("we," "us"). This policy explains what information Dock collects, how it's used, and your choices, in plain terms that match exactly what the app actually does.

The short version

Everything Dock tracks, including your paired tag, lock history, modes, schedules, and blocked-app lists, stays on your device. We don't run any server and there's no Dock account. Data only leaves your phone in four cases, each handled by Google: ads in the free version (Google AdMob), purchases (Google Play), Google Drive backup if you turn it on, and anonymous usage statistics and crash reports (Google Firebase), which you can turn off. Which apps or websites you block never leaves your phone.

What Dock stores, and where

All of the following is stored locally on your device, in Dock's own private app storage:

  • Your paired NFC tag's ID: so Dock can recognize your tag on future taps.
  • Dock/undock history: timestamps and durations of each session, used to build the Activity tab's charts and averages.
  • Modes, schedules, and your blocked app/website selections: whatever you've configured.
  • Your theme and purchases: which theme you've applied, and which themes or upgrades you own, so Dock can unlock them.
  • Blocked notifications: if a notification arrives from a blocked app while you're docked, Dock records that app's name, the notification's title and text, and when it arrived, so you can see what you missed without unblocking the app. This is cleared automatically at the start of your next lock.

Uninstalling Dock deletes all of it from your device. If your phone's own Android backup is turned on, Android may include Dock's app data in your device backup, stored by Google in your Google account, the same as it does for other apps; you control this in your phone's backup settings.

Ads (free version)

The free version of Dock shows banner ads served by Google AdMob. To deliver and measure ads, Google's ads software collects information such as your device's advertising ID, IP address, and basic device and app-usage information. This goes to Google, not to us. Dock Pro removes ads.

You can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings (Settings → Google → Ads, or Settings → Privacy → Ads). Learn how Google uses this information.

Usage statistics and crash reports

To find bugs and learn which features are worth improving, Dock uses Google Firebase:

  • Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics): if Dock crashes, a report is sent with where in the app it crashed, your device model, Android version, and Dock's version.
  • Usage statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase): anonymous counts of which features are used, such as docking (and whether by tag, by holding the button, or by a schedule), emergency undocks, saving a mode or schedule, which theme is applied, turning on backup, purchases, and which screens are opened. Firebase also collects basic device information, an app-instance identifier, and approximate location from your IP address.

These never include which apps or websites you block, your mode, schedule or custom theme names, your notifications, your paired tag, or anything you type.

This data goes to Google Firebase, where we can see it only as totals and trends, not tied to who you are. You can turn usage statistics off at any time in Dock → Settings → Share usage stats. Crash reports stay on so we can fix problems. Learn how Google handles this data.

Purchases

Dock Pro, themes, and Emergency Undock purchases are processed entirely by Google Play. Dock never sees or stores your payment details. Google Play tells Dock what you've bought, so Dock can unlock it and restore it if you reinstall.

Google Drive backup (optional, off by default)

If you turn on Google Drive backup in Settings, Dock saves one small file to your own Google Drive containing your modes, schedules, chosen mode, applied theme, and paired tag's ID. Your lock history and blocked notifications are never backed up.

  • Where it's stored: in Google Drive's hidden app data folder. It doesn't appear in your Drive file list, and Dock can only see its own file there, never your other files.
  • Who can see it: only you, through Dock. The file travels directly between your phone and your Google Drive; it never passes through us.
  • Your Google account: you pick the account with Android's account picker. Dock keeps that account's email address on your device, only to show you where your backup is.
  • Turning it off: deletes the backup file from your Drive. You can also remove Dock's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Step-by-step instructions are on the delete your data page.

Dock's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. That information is used only to back up and restore your Dock setup, is never sold, never used for advertising, and never shared with anyone.

Permissions Dock uses, and why

  • NFC: to read your physical tag and trigger a lock/unlock.
  • Accessibility Service: this is the permission that lets Dock actually block apps and enforce Strict Mode. Specifically, it reads the package name of whichever app is in the foreground, to check it against your blocked list, and for web browsers specifically, reads the address bar's URL to check it against your blocked websites list. It does not read message content, keystrokes, passwords, or any other on-screen text or content. It only reads the foreground app's identity and, for browsers, the URL bar. None of this leaves your device.
  • Notifications (post + listener access): to detect and record notifications from blocked apps while you're docked (see "Blocked notifications" above), and to show Dock's own status notifications.
  • Schedule exact alarm: so scheduled locks/unlocks fire at the exact minute you set, rather than being delayed by the system.
  • Boot completed: to re-arm your schedules after your phone restarts.
  • Internet and advertising ID: for ads in the free version, purchases, Google Drive backup, and usage statistics and crash reports, as described above.

What Dock does not do

  • No Dock account or sign-in is needed. Google Drive backup uses your existing Google account, only if you turn it on.
  • No tracking across other apps or websites, and no advertising profile of our own.
  • No data is sold.
  • Which apps or websites you block, your mode and schedule names, and your notifications are never sent anywhere.

Children's privacy

Dock is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect data from children.

Changes to this policy
